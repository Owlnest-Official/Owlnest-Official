OWLNEST Lab

What shapes our ability
to focus and rest?

Why can it take so long to unwind after work or feel awake in the morning? OWLNEST Lab follows research into these everyday transitions and considers how our surroundings—and future products—might help.

Our first area of study: sleep

Current research area

Sleep Lab

Sleep Lab focuses on the habits, surroundings, and experiences of sleep. Our initial work looks at nighttime light and how people use Lume at home.

Inside Sleep Lab ↗

How we evaluate a product idea

We review independent findings, assess the quality and limits of the evidence, and consider whether an idea could become a useful product. Feedback from use helps guide further work, including reports of no change or difficulty using a design.

Where our research could lead

Future research directions

Feeling alert, staying focused, unwinding, resting, sleeping, and recovering from activity.

These are questions for our long-term exploration—not a range of launched products or established research programs. Each direction needs a specific question, an appropriate method and its own evidence.

How scientists study waking

Research into neural circuits asks different questions from a bedside lamp. Adamantidis and colleagues, including Karl Deisseroth, used genetically targeted neurons and light delivered inside the brains of mice to study waking. This is optogenetics—not ordinary room lighting.

This animal study is not evidence for Lume, an OWLNEST technology, a collaboration or an endorsement.
