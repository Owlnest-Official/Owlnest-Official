Evidence / Light after dark

Your evening light
may matter more than you think

Bedtime advice often focuses on what you do. Research also points to the light around you.

What independent research tells us about evening light—and why OWLNEST starts with the environment.

Human laboratory research · Gooley, 2011

Room light can suppress melatonin before bed

In 116 healthy young adults, evening room light suppressed melatonin and shortened its duration compared with dim light.

This was a controlled laboratory analysis, not a Lume trial or a promise that changing one lamp will improve sleep.

Gooley et al. · 2011 · Read the paper ↗

The OWLNEST view

What stays on
when your day is over?

The workday ends, but the same overhead lights may still be on. We think evening deserves as much thought as the tasks that came before it.

Human crossover study · Rahman, 2017

The same illuminance can produce different responses

The eye helps the body tell time as well as see. Its response depends on the light’s composition, not only its visual brightness.

In a 16-person study, two light sources at 50 lux produced different melatonin suppression and alertness responses. Total sleep time and sleep efficiency did not differ significantly.

The finding concerns those study conditions and light sources—not Lume.

Rahman, St Hilaire & Lockley · 2017 ↗

Warm color tells only part of the story

A lamp can look right for the evening. We believe its design should consider more than appearance. That is why Lume begins with a specially tuned sleep spectrum.

Real-home observations · Cain, 2020

Evening light and sleep in real homes

Greater evening light exposure, relative to a person’s own average, was associated with more wakefulness in the first 90 minutes after bedtime.

An association is not proof of cause. Individual light sensitivity varied; this was not a test of Lume.

Cain et al. · 2020 · Read the paper ↗

The OWLNEST view

Start with the light
you already use

A bedside light is something you use almost without thinking. We want that familiar object to reflect what research tells us about light after dark, without adding complicated controls to the evening.

Expert consensus · Brown, 2022

Light recommendations change from day to night

Day

Seek appropriately timed, ample light.

Evening

Reduce light exposure as bedtime approaches.

Sleep

Keep the environment as dark as possible.

Guidance for healthy adults with regular daytime schedules. It is not a lamp certification or a measured Lume performance standard.

Brown et al. · 2022 · Read the recommendations ↗

Understanding the evidence

What each kind of evidence can tell us

Published research
Independent studies inform the questions we ask. They do not establish Lume-specific sleep or biological outcomes.
Product design rationale
Lume’s specially tuned sleep spectrum is a design choice for nighttime—not a guarantee of melatonin production or better sleep.
Product measurements
Product comparisons require measured spectral output and defined exposure conditions. This page makes no quantified Lume performance or superiority claim. White-light CCT rankings alone are not appropriate for Lume’s saturated orange-red light.
Participant research
Sleep Lab records everyday experiences, including no change and negative feedback. A questionnaire is not a hormone measurement or clinical trial. Explore the work ↗
