# 4. BRAND WORLD

Owlnest is:

Made for life after dark.

The visual identity should explore what a brand built specifically around NIGHTTIME LIVING could look like.

Do not interpret nighttime only as:

black background + orange light.

Think more deeply.

Possible conceptual material includes:

- the transition from day to night
- darkness as negative space
- artificial light interrupting darkness
- pools of light
- silhouettes
- shadows
- after-hours domestic life
- quiet architecture
- nocturnal rituals
- late-night editorial photography
- light as a physical material
- the spectrum hidden inside visible color
- the moment a room changes character after sunset

These are creative starting points, not mandatory motifs.

Invent stronger ideas if you have them.

---

# 5. LUME SHOULD BE A VISUAL CHARACTER

Lume has a highly recognizable physical form:

- compact egg-shaped illuminated body
- round wooden base
- deep amber / orange-red nighttime glow

Do not treat it merely as a product image inside a rectangle.

Explore ways the product itself could drive composition.

Examples might include:

- scale contrast
- darkness interrupted by one pool of Lume light
- extreme crop
- silhouette
- reflected light
- object emerging from negative space
- light crossing section boundaries
- product anchoring typography
- visual transitions driven by the lamp's glow

These are examples only.

Do not limit yourself to them.

---

# 6. EDITORIAL, NOT TEMPLATE

Owlnest should have the confidence of an editorial object.

Explore:

- asymmetry
- dramatic type scale
- unexpected crop
- narrow text columns
- oversized statements
- captions
- marginal notes
- numbered research annotations
- vertical rhythm
- deliberate visual interruptions
- cinematic pacing

But do not make the website difficult to use.

The creativity should strengthen comprehension, not sacrifice it.

---

# 7. SCIENCE AND LAB CAN HAVE VISUAL CHARACTER

Do not make Science simply "dark page with science cards."

Do not make Evidence simply "more cards with labels."

Explore distinctive visual systems for different roles.

For example:

Science might feel like:
an editorial explanation of invisible light.

Evidence might feel like:
an evidence ledger / annotated archive / transparent record.

Lab might feel like:
an active living research system.

21-Day Program might feel like:
participation in a living field study.

Products might feel like:
a tactile product encounter.

Homepage might feel like:
entering Owlnest's world after dark.

These are directions, not mandatory layouts.

---

# 8. MOTION IS ALLOWED

You may propose meaningful motion.

Examples:

- light gradually replacing daylight
- typography reacting subtly to scroll
- product emerging from darkness
- spectral transitions
- section illumination
- cinematic image reveals
- sticky editorial storytelling
- restrained scroll choreography

Avoid motion for decoration alone.

Avoid making content depend on animation to remain readable.

Respect reduced-motion users.

---

# 9. CHINESE DOES NOT NEED TO VISUALLY IMITATE ENGLISH

English and Traditional Chinese must communicate equivalent meaning.

But their typography may be art-directed differently.

The Chinese experience should feel equally intentional and premium.

You may propose:

- different display-font strategy
- different line lengths
- different breakpoints
- different headline composition
- different vertical rhythm

Do not force Chinese into English-shaped boxes.

---

Preserve the same information and functionality.
