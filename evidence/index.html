General mechanisms

Peer-reviewed research and recognized measurement standards explain how light, timing, intensity, duration, and spectrum can contribute to non-visual responses.

Supports general scientific context. Does not measure Lume.

Design rationale

Research-informed direction

Describes design intent. Does not establish a measured human outcome.

Claim → Evidence Map

What supports what?

Claim

The body uses light as a timing cue.

Support: Established human research on circadian and non-visual light responses.

Claim

Warm appearance does not fully describe spectrum.

Support: SPD is the measurement of output across wavelengths. Different lamps can look warm without sharing one universal spectrum.

Claim

Lume uses a specially tuned sleep spectrum.

Support: Product design specification and design intent. A public measured spectral profile is still pending.

Claims not made

A guaranteed or clinical sleep outcome.

Support required: Product-specific participant data or controlled human research appropriate to the strength of the claim. That evidence is not currently public.

Source audit

Primary research used here

Each source supports a general mechanism or measurement concept. None is a test of Lume.

Evening room light and melatonin timing

Gooley et al., 2011 · human laboratory study. Supports the relevance of room light before bedtime; not a Lume outcome.

PubMed record

Spectral tuning of evening ambient light

Rahman et al., 2017 · controlled comparison of blue-depleted and conventional evening light. Supports spectrum as a design consideration; not a Lume comparison.

Full text

Real-world evening home lighting

Cain et al., 2020 · home light measurements and individual sensitivity. Supports the importance of exposure conditions and variation between people.

Scientific Reports

Indoor light exposure recommendations

Brown et al., 2022 · expert recommendations for healthy adults. Supports lowering evening and nighttime exposure; not a fixed Lume usage threshold.

PLOS Biology

Human action spectrum and CIE measurement framework

Brainard et al., 2001 and CIE S 026 explain wavelength sensitivity and how biologically relevant light can be quantified. They do not provide Lume values.

Future measurement

What responsible Lume evidence should disclose

  • Actual product and output setting
  • SPD and instrument or method
  • Distance, angle, illuminance, and room conditions
  • Named comparison lamp and same-condition protocol
  • Measurement date and relevant melanopic metrics
Current boundary

What Owlnest does not claim

Lume is not a medical device and is not presented as a treatment for insomnia or another sleep disorder. Owlnest does not claim a clinical result, guaranteed melatonin response, circadian reset, or guaranteed improvement in sleep.

General research cannot establish how one person will respond to one product in one room.
