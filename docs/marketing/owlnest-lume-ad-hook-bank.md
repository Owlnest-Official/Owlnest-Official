## 1. Hook Bank Rules

* Hooks can borrow attention from adjacent problems people already care about.
* Body copy must return to sleep, blue light, melatonin, night mode, and bedtime light.
* Do not imply Lume directly causes weight loss, muscle growth, skin repair, productivity improvement, anxiety relief, or medical outcomes.
* Send traffic to the Products page first.
* Build landing pages only after winning hooks appear.
* Keep the core product claim steady: deep amber, low-blue sleep-spectrum light for better nights.
* Traditional Chinese ad copy should sound natural for Taiwan readers: direct, warm, not stiff, not medical.

## 8. Red Line Checklist

Before launching any ad, review:

* Does the hook borrow attention without changing product positioning?
* Does the body copy return to sleep-spectrum light?
* Does it mention bedtime light, blue light, melatonin, night mode, or the room signal?
* Does it avoid direct outcome claims?
* Does it avoid medical, weight loss, beauty, muscle growth, productivity, or mental health claims?
* Does it avoid claiming Lume directly causes weight loss, performance gains, skin repair, anxiety relief, memory improvement, or guaranteed sleep?
* Does the Traditional Chinese avoid stiff translated wording and prohibited terms?
* Does the ad send traffic to the Products page first unless a winning angle already exists?
* Does the CTA stay product-centered instead of promising an adjacent outcome?
* Would the ad still be accurate if a customer only buys a sleep-spectrum lamp?
