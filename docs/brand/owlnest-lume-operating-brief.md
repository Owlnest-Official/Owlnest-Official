## Research and claims

Sleep Lab seeks who fits, when value exists, where it is limited, and who may not fit—not universal effectiveness. Keep positive, neutral, unchanged, and negative feedback.

The 21-Day Program uses Day 0 baseline, Day 7/14 check-ins, Day 21 review, Day 30 follow-up. Public CTAs describe the program; application configuration is protected.

Do not present conceptual spectrum visuals as measured product data. General research is design context, not Lume clinical validation. Never invent measurements, comparisons, participants, personas, testimonials, or outcomes.

Lume is not a medical device. No insomnia treatment, medical efficacy, guaranteed sleep or melatonin outcomes, or all-night sleep-benefit claims. Use after dark; switch off at bedtime unless light is still needed. Preserve real safety instructions.

## Permanent copy rule

Delete before adding. One section, one idea. Let the headline carry the message; supporting copy is usually 1–2 short sentences. Delete repetition, keep deeper information with its owner, and use imagery and whitespace instead of filler.

Public site should present what is real and ready today. Omit nonexistent content rather than displaying placeholders such as coming soon, currently unavailable, results pending, future results, 即將, 尚未, or 敬請期待. Do not hide necessary operational errors or legal disclosures.

EN/ZH retain identical architecture, imagery, CTA logic, and facts. Chinese is natural Taiwan usage, not literal translation.
